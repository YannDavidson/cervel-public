This repository uses the **Apache License, Version 2.0** for material published here unless a file or directory clearly states a different license.

The goal is to make the deliberately public CERVEL interoperability surface usable by developers while keeping the boundary between this repository and non-public CERVEL technology explicit.

## What is licensed

The Apache-2.0 license applies to material actually published in this repository, including future executable reference tooling, SDK code, validators, examples, schemas, and documentation unless a more specific notice accompanies particular material.

A public license applies only to material that has actually been released here. It does not make non-public CERVEL source code, designs, services, product implementations, or other unpublished material available under this license.

Repository proximity, naming similarity, documentation references, interoperability with a public interface, or compatibility with a public contract do not by themselves place non-public material under this repository's license.

## Patent scope

Apache-2.0 includes an express patent license. The legally operative scope is the scope stated in Section 3 of the license. In general, that grant applies only to patent claims licensable by a Contributor that are necessarily infringed by that Contributor's Contribution alone or by combination of the Contribution with the Work to which the Contribution was submitted.

This policy does not add to, subtract from, narrow, expand, reserve around, or otherwise modify the patent license granted by Apache-2.0. If this explanatory document conflicts with the license text, the license text controls.

Publishing material under Apache-2.0 can therefore have patent consequences when a Contributor controls patent claims necessarily infringed by the published Contribution or its relevant combination with the Work. Maintainers should not assume that keeping an implementation private prevents a patent grant for claims necessarily infringed by material that is actually contributed and published here.

Conversely, publication of one interface, schema, document, example, validator, SDK component, or reference implementation does not by itself publish every related CERVEL implementation or grant rights in unrelated patent claims. The actual scope depends on Section 3 and the particular Contribution and Work.

For that reason, potentially patent-relevant material MUST complete disclosure and IP review **before it is pushed to a public branch or otherwise publicly disclosed**, not merely before merge. Material that would expose a protected implementation mechanism, newly developed inventive concept, or claim-sensitive architecture should remain non-public unless publication has been deliberately approved.

## Publication boundary

The public/private boundary is a publication-control rule, not an attempted exception to Apache-2.0.

Once material is intentionally published here under Apache-2.0, maintainers should not rely on `LICENSING.md`, private-repository status elsewhere, or internal labels to retract or contract rights already granted under the license. The control point is therefore the decision about **what enters this public repository at all**.

Public releases should expose only material approved for public interoperability and developer use. Non-public implementations should remain outside this repository unless separately reviewed and deliberately released.

This repository is a curated public interoperability and developer surface. The license does not imply publication of private CERVEL implementation details, including private knowledge-compilation logic, permission or authority engines, production synchronization or cryptographic mechanisms, private provenance machinery, model-routing internals, control-plane implementation, unreleased research, or other material not actually distributed in this repository.

This statement describes what CERVEL intends to keep outside the public Work. It is not an additional restriction on material that has actually been licensed under Apache-2.0 and must not be read as modifying the rights granted by the license.

Licensing a draft does not make it stable. Protocol documents marked experimental or draft remain experimental and may change incompatibly according to their published stability statements.

Legal questions about patent scope, ownership, contribution rights, or the relationship between a planned public release and patent claims should be reviewed by qualified counsel before publication.
